Solar neutrinos between 2 and 5 MeV: where is the data?

11 October 2026 · 2 min read · solar-neutrinos, MSW, detectors

The $^8$B branch of the solar neutrino spectrum reaches about 15 MeV, and the electron-neutrino survival probability $P_{ee}(E)$ across it is one of the cleanest demonstrations of matter effects in particle physics. At low energy the oscillation is vacuum-dominated and averages to $P_{ee}\approx0.55$. At high energy the matter-dominated branch gives $P_{ee}\approx0.30$. The question for this post: where does the transition between the two happen, and who has actually measured it?

Where the transition sits

For the density near the $^8$B production zone, the 1-2 resonance condition lands at roughly 1 to 2 MeV, and the visible rise of $P_{ee}$ toward lower energy (the "upturn") plays out over a few MeV above that. The derivation is in the next post. The upshot is that the 2 to 5 MeV region is where vacuum and matter terms are comparable, which makes it the region where $P_{ee}$ is most sensitive to anything that modifies the effective matter potential or adds another level to the problem.

Why running detectors struggle there

Real-time detectors have a hard time covering this range from either side.

  • Water Cherenkov (Super-Kamiokande, SNO): analysis thresholds sit at a few MeV, set by radioactivity in the water and surrounding materials and by PMT noise.
  • Liquid scintillator (Borexino, KamLAND): the low end of the $^8$B range is crowded by cosmogenic $^{11}$C from muon spallation on $^{12}$C and by external gamma backgrounds.

Radiochemical experiments measure integrated rates, so they cannot resolve a spectral shape at all. The result is a region where the shape of $P_{ee}(E)$ is constrained mostly through global fits and extrapolation, not through a clean, event-by-event spectrum.

Why is that important for the new physics

Sterile-neutrino admixture and non-standard interactions both modify the effective potential or add a level, and both would show up as a distortion in exactly the transition region. A shape measurement there would test the LMA-MSW prediction more directly than the high-energy or low-energy plateaus can.

A detector with a charged-current channel that opens at 1.44 MeV could in principle cover the whole range. That is the motivation for looking at deuterium, covered in a later post.

What is still unclear

How much the gap has narrowed with the latest low-threshold analyses is something to be checked against current literature before relying on this post. Also open: how much of the remaining spectral freedom in this region is already excluded by global fits, as opposed to simply not measured directly.
